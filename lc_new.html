Platforms

Show up where it counts

Each of the three platforms reaches a different slice of the Lucky Crow community, tapping into some of the learnings from the community survey. The focus right now is building a presence and following before the doors open.

Instagram
Primary Channel

This is where Lucky Crow's audience already lives. Book lovers, wellness-minded regulars, and third-space seekers are all here, across a broad age range.

What it's for

  • Building hype and community support before the doors open
  • Teasing out brand identity and interior design (behind the scenes)
  • Addressing what we've heard from the community

Content types

  • Vibe and atmosphere visuals
  • Community-informed updates
  • Engagement posts (naming contests, guessing games)
  • Brand storytelling

Best practices

  • Polished but not perfect. Intentional, phone-captured content often outperforms over-produced content.
  • Reels get the most reach and outperform static for new audiences.
  • Consistency beats frequency. A realistic cadence instead of sporadic bursts.
  • Use location tags and keywords in captions for local discovery.
  • Stories are great for behind-the-scenes content.
Facebook
Community Anchor

Facebook reaches the people most active in local community groups, which is where a lot of our research came from.

What it's for

  • Longer-form storytelling and direct dialogue with Madison
  • Turning survey findings into visible action
  • Where community listening becomes community building

Content types

  • Community acknowledgment posts
  • Founder story and origin content
  • Local group engagement

Best practices

  • Longer-form storytelling and community dialogue.
  • The algorithm rewards comments and replies. Ask questions and respond as much as you can.
  • Drop links in the first comment, not the caption, to avoid reach penalties.
  • Hit Facebook Groups for focused communities (but you already know this).
  • 80/20 rule: 80% value-driven content, 20% promotional.
TikTok
Targeted Reach

A smaller but valuable audience, specifically the reading community on BookTok, which overlaps directly with Lucky Crow's core demographic.

What it's for

  • Short, authentic "build in the open" content
  • Reaching a passionate, tightly-knit niche
  • Showing personality before the space exists

Content types

  • Short-form brand story
  • Drink and product reveals
  • Quick educational content

Best practices

  • Raw and real outperforms polished. Think phone camera, natural light, genuine energy.
  • Hook in the first 3 seconds to capture and keep attention. Use messaging on screen.
  • Respond to as many comments as you can with video replies to build community and spark new content.
  • Participate in trends only when they genuinely fit.
Content Pillars

Lucky Crow focus areas

Every post focuses on one of six areas, designed to inform, entertain, and engage, while steadily building the brand, the audience, and the anticipation.

01
Building something special
Space search updates, mood board teasers, logo significance, waitlist milestones.

Content examples

  • Mood board teasers: glimpses of design inspiration without revealing the full space
  • Logo significance: the meaning and symbolism behind the mark
  • Waitlist milestones: "100+ people on the list!" and a reminder on how to join
  • Location updates: signed lease and location info
02
Background
Founder origin story, personal milestones, and the story behind Lucky Crow told in Katy's own words.

Content examples

  • Meet Katy: a casual, personal introduction
  • The Leap: the story of how you decided to pursue Lucky Crow
  • Mom's story: her inspiration, and a tease of the cookie fundraising to honor her memory
03
About Lucky Crow
Everything people want to and should know about Lucky Crow, and what makes it unlike anything else in Madison.

Content examples

  • "Step into the Crow's Nest": Katy answers questions she anticipates or has been asked
  • Apothecary and Readeasy explainers: why the space leans into those names and what they mean
  • Secret entrance: building intrigue by teasing how guests access the Readeasy
  • Madison context: what Lucky Crow sees in the community and why now is the right time
04
Food & Drink
Weekly drink reveals, contests, Lotus spotlights, plant-based education, and food menu content.

Content examples

  • Weekly drink reveal: a stylized photo introducing one new drink each week
  • "Name the Drink" contest: winner gets drinks for a year, a shoutout, and a merch bundle
  • Lotus brand feature: spotlighting Lotus as the only place in Madison to get it
  • Plant-based energy 101: a short educational series demystifying botanical energy drinks
  • The whole menu: highlighting snacks and food items beyond drinks
05
Reading & Books
"Book of the Week" picks, book and drink pairings, and deeper Readeasy storytelling.

Content examples

  • Book of the Week: Katy shares a pick that sets the tone for the week, or asks the community for recommendations
  • Book chatter: reading and book-related prompts and questions designed to spark conversation and community engagement
  • Book + drink pairing: matching a book's vibe to a specific drink
  • Deeper on Readeasy: the inspiration behind the lounge concept and celebrating it as the first and only one in the world
06
Trend Awareness
Keeping an eye on what trends are popular on social and finding moments to jump in when it feels right.

Content examples

  • Trending audio: using a popular sound in a way that feels natural to the brand
  • Format participation: joining a trending format (challenge, POV, Netflix Doc, etc.) with a Lucky Crow spin
  • Timely hooks: tying relevant cultural moments or seasons to Lucky Crow content
Considerations
CTA

Every post should drive people to take action. Join the waitlist, follow accounts on all platforms, come visit, etc. Keep the ask soft and varied, but make sure it's usually there.

Cross-platform creative

Not every piece of content needs to be built from scratch for each platform. A lot of what gets created can be used directly across multiple channels, or adapted with minor tweaks.

Trend awareness

Keep an eye on what's moving on social and look for moments to jump in, but only when it feels on-brand.

Production efficiencies

Look for opportunities to produce multiple pieces of content, for one or multiple series, in a single session.

Engagement

Responding to comments, asking questions, and inviting the community to weigh in builds relationships and extends reach. Every interaction is an opportunity to grow the audience and deepen the connection.

Cross-platform creative in action
One post, four platforms

When setting up for a shoot or a video, think beyond one platform. A single setup can generate content for all channels, with minimal extra effort. The more you can get out of each session, the further your content goes.
