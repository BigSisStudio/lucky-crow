Social Strategy

Lucky Crow Apothecary & Readeasy™ · 07.27.2026

Platforms

Show up where it counts

Each of the three platforms reaches a different slice of the Lucky Crow community, tapping into some of the learnings from the community survey. The focus right now is building a presence and following before the doors open.

Instagram
Primary Channel

This is where Lucky Crow's audience already lives. Book lovers, wellness-minded regulars, and third-space seekers are all here, across a broad age range.

What it's for
  • →Building hype and community support before the doors open
  • →Teasing out brand identity and interior design (behind the scenes)
  • →Addressing what we've heard from the community
Best practices
  • →Polished but not perfect. Intentional, phone-captured content often outperforms over-produced content.
  • →Reels get the most reach and outperform static for new audiences.
  • →Consistency beats frequency. A realistic cadence instead of sporadic bursts.
  • →Use location tags and keywords in captions for local discovery.
  • →Stories are great for behind-the-scenes content.
Facebook
Community Anchor

Facebook reaches the people most active in local community groups, which is where a lot of our research came from.

What it's for
  • →Longer-form storytelling and direct dialogue with Madison
  • →Turning survey findings into visible action
  • →Where community listening becomes community building
Best practices
  • →Longer-form storytelling and community dialogue.
  • →The algorithm rewards comments and replies. Ask questions and respond as much as you can.
  • →Drop links in the first comment, not the caption, to avoid reach penalties.
  • →Hit Facebook Groups for focused communities (but you already know this).
  • →80/20 rule: 80% value-driven content, 20% promotional.
TikTok
Targeted Reach

A smaller but valuable audience, specifically the reading community on BookTok, which overlaps directly with Lucky Crow's core demographic.

What it's for
  • →Short, authentic "build in the open" content
  • →Reaching a passionate, tightly-knit niche
  • →Showing personality before the space exists
Best practices
  • →Raw and real outperforms polished. Think phone camera, natural light, genuine energy.
  • →Hook in the first 3 seconds to capture and keep attention. Use messaging on screen.
  • →Respond to as many comments as you can with video replies to build community and spark new content.
  • →Participate in trends only when they genuinely fit.

Survey Review & Recommendations

Lucky Crow Apothecary & Readeasy™ · Presentation 02 · 06.09.2026

Platform

Survey selection

We explored multiple platforms based on the criteria we discussed.

Tally
  • →Free, unlimited forms and submissions
  • →Clean, mobile-first interface with no branding on free plan
  • →Native email capture, QR code generation, and conditional logic
Typeform
  • →Conversational one-question-at-a-time format
  • →~47% avg completion rate vs 21.5% industry average
  • →Free tier caps at 10 responses/month; $50/mo to remove branding
SurveyMonkey
  • →Widely recognized and easy to use
  • →Strong analytics and reporting tools
  • →Free plan caps at 25 responses per survey
Google Forms
  • →Free and unlimited responses
  • →Integrates directly with Google Sheets
  • →No branding options, generic appearance
Recommendation: Tally

Tally is the right platform for this survey. It's free with unlimited responses, generates a QR code for flyer distribution, captures email natively, and is simple enough for Katy to manage solo. Conditional logic also allows turning off reward redemption after a set number of responses.

Survey Questions

14 questions. Under 2 minutes.

Every question earns its place. Each has a rationale — what we're learning and why it matters for Lucky Crow's opening strategy.

Q1 — Location
What neighborhood do you live in or spend the most time in?
Maps demand to known pockets of the target audience. Helps confirm or refine location decisions.
Q2 — Third Space Habits
How often do you work, study, or spend time at a coffee shop or similar space?
Validates the third-space concept and sizes the frequent-visitor segment.
Q3 — Pain Points
What's missing from the coffee shops or third spaces you currently use?
Open-ended. Surfaces unmet needs that Lucky Crow can directly address in positioning.
Q4 — Hours
What hours would you most likely visit a space like Lucky Crow?
Directly informs operating hours decisions. The "later hours" differentiator was already surfacing in community conversations.
Q5 — Drink Preferences
What types of drinks do you typically order at a coffee shop or café?
Helps calibrate menu priorities and understand familiarity with plant-based energy drinks.
Q6 — Energy Drink Awareness
Are you familiar with plant-based energy drinks like Lotus Energy?
Baseline awareness check. Shapes how much education Lucky Crow needs to do pre-opening.
Q7 — Reading Habits
How often do you read for pleasure?
Validates the Readeasy concept and sizes the reading-community segment.
Q8 — Reading Environment
Where do you usually read?
Reveals whether a dedicated reading space fills a real gap or competes with home habits.
Q9 — Seating Preferences
What kind of seating do you prefer when working or reading in a public space?
Directly informs interior design decisions. Cozy seating was already a recurring community ask.
Q10 — Noise Level
What's your preferred noise level when working or reading in a public space?
Helps calibrate ambiance. Quiet vs. ambient music vs. background chatter affects space design and policy.
Q11 — Pets & Kids
Would a pet-friendly or kid-friendly policy matter to you?
Informs policy decisions that affect a significant segment of the Madison market.
Q12 — Social Media
Which social platforms do you use most?
Directly informs the social strategy — where to show up and with what content format.
Q13 — Waitlist Interest
Would you be interested in getting early access updates about Lucky Crow?
Converts survey respondents into a warm list. Yes/No keeps friction low.
Q14 — Email Capture & Incentive
As a thank you for taking the survey, we'd love to give you half off your first drink at Lucky Crow when it opens, plus a free premium sticker.
Converts survey respondents into an early-access list. The incentive rewards participation and creates a reason to engage at opening.

Survey Strategy Kickoff

Lucky Crow Apothecary & Readeasy™ · Presentation 01 · 06.02.2026

Kickoff meeting agenda and thinking going into the community survey strategy. Five topics covering the approach, what we wanted to learn, who we were asking, how to incentivize responses, and how to distribute.

Topic 1 — Survey Approach
What are we trying to learn, and how do we ask it?

Aligning on the core questions the survey needs to answer, what decisions it needs to inform, and how to frame questions that get honest, actionable responses.

Topic 2 — Learning Goals
What do we want to walk away knowing?

Defining the specific insights that will inform Lucky Crow's opening strategy — location, hours, menu, atmosphere, and positioning.

Topic 3 — Audience
Who are we asking?

Identifying the right respondent profile — the Madison community members most likely to become Lucky Crow regulars — and how to reach them.

Topic 4 — Incentives
What's the reward for taking the survey?

Determining the right incentive to drive completion — a discount on the first drink, a sticker, or early access — and how to structure the reward capture without adding friction.

Topic 5 — Distribution
How do we get it in front of people?

Planning the distribution strategy — physical flyers with QR codes, social media, community groups, and local partners — to maximize reach with the right audience.

After this meeting: we write the questions, recommend a platform, then design the survey and the promotional piece.
