TOPICS

Let's talk

01
Approach

The Mom Test is a question framework that avoids pitching your idea while gathering feedback — instead of asking "would you use this?" (which invites polite lies), it asks about real past behavior and actual problems people have already experienced. The goal is to surface honest signal, not validation.

We will also be using format, set-up language and survey-length best practices to ensure people see the survey through.

02
What are we trying to learn?
  1. If this survey came back tomorrow with 100 responses, what are the three things you'd most want to know?
  2. Is there anything about your customers or concept that you're not sure about yet? Is there anything that would change if some of your assumptions don't align?
  3. With such a unique concept, are you okay learning separately about needs for a local plant-based apothecary and for a special place to get lost in for events, reading or work?
  4. For the feedback you received about "Traction," what type of information do you think the investors or lenders want to see? Email signups, proof of interest, demographic data, something else?
  5. Beyond email addresses, what data points would actually help you: demographics, location, beverage habits, event interest, willingness to pay?
  6. Are there things you feel confident enough about that you don't need to survey for them — so we don't waste questions?
03
Who are we asking?
  1. Do you have an existing audience anywhere or are you part of any groups you'd like to tap into? IRL or digital.
  2. Who would you like to hear from? Readers, students, people who live in certain neighborhoods, etc?
  3. Are there any groups you specifically don't want to skew toward? People who would give biased or watered down feedback, or don't fall into any of the target demographics we're looking into, can do more harm than help.
04
What's the trade for filling out the survey?
  1. What can we offer someone for a few minutes of their time? Win a prize, a free drink, invite to grand opening, branded cup, etc?
  2. In addition to capturing email addresses for a newsletter, how else can we take advantage of engaged potential customers?
05
How does the survey get in front of people?
  1. We've discussed flyers, online groups, email lists and more. Let's identify 2–3 approaches that will yield the best results.
  2. Are there any communities, organizations, or venues in Madison you already have a relationship with that might help share it?
  3. Would you be comfortable doing any cold or blind outreach, including posting in Madison Reddit, Facebook groups, or UW forums?
