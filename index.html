PART 1 — METHODOLOGY

The Mom Test approach

All survey questions are written using The Mom Test — a framework designed to eliminate bias by asking about real past behavior instead of hypothetical preferences. The goal is honest signal, not validation.

Core principles

  • → Ask about past behavior, not future intent
  • → Never telegraph what you're hoping to hear
  • → Open-ended questions surface real language
  • → Closed questions confirm hypotheses
  • → Both types are needed

Survey specs

  • → 14 questions total (including Q1b)
  • → Estimated 5–7 minutes to complete
  • → Multiple choice, scale, open field, two-part
  • → Demographics placed at end per best practice
  • → Designed mobile-first for QR code traffic
PART 2 — SURVEY QUESTIONS

All 14 questions

All questions are finalized unless marked as pending. Source and rationale provided for each.

Section 1 — The Third Space
Q1 — Where Do You Go?
When you need to get out of the house to read, study, work, or meet up with someone, where do you go in Madison? Select all that apply.
Opens with a behavior-first anchor. Captures where people actually go today rather than hypothetical preferences. Sets up context for Q2 and Q3.
  • → Coffee shop or café
  • → Library
  • → Bookstore
  • → Restaurant
  • → Juice bar
  • → Other (please specify)
Q1b — Frequency
How often do you visit these kinds of places?
Frequency data reveals how habitual these visits are. A weekly visitor is a very different customer than a monthly one.
  • → More than once a week
  • → Once a week
  • → Every other week
  • → Once a month
  • → Less than once a month
Q2 — What Brings You Back?
What brings you back to a place? Select up to three.
Replaces Katy's original "Vibe Killer" question. Flipping to what brings you back captures a fuller picture of what people value most. Capping at three forces prioritization and makes data more actionable.
  • → Food or beverage quality / selection
  • → Noise level / Vibe or atmosphere
  • → Decor or design / Staff or service
  • → Price / Cleanliness / Kid-friendly
  • → Outdoor seating / Comfortable seating / Privacy
  • → Other (please specify)
Q3 — Travel Distance
How far are you willing to travel for a spot you really love?
Helps understand realistic catchment area and informs location decisions. Madison residents can be very neighborhood-loyal.
  • → Under 10 minutes
  • → 10–20 minutes
  • → 20–30 minutes
  • → I'd go across town if it was worth it
Section 2 — Drinks & Caffeine Habits
Q4 — Caffeine Routine
On a typical week, what does your caffeine routine look like?
Adapted from Katy's original. Separates frequency and preference, and distinguishes traditional energy drinks from plant-based — critical for understanding where the audience is today vs. where Lucky Crow takes them.
  • → Mostly coffee
  • → Mostly traditional energy drinks (Red Bull, Monster, etc.)
  • → Mostly plant-based energy drinks (Lotus, Guayakí, etc.)
  • → Mix of different types
  • → I avoid caffeine entirely
  • → Other
Q5 — Plant-Based Experience
Have you ever tried a plant-based or botanical energy drink, like Lotus, Guayakí, or similar?
Katy's original asked a hypothetical choosing scenario which primes a yes. This version asks about past behavior first. More honest signal, less social desirability bias.
  • → Yes, it's a regular thing for me
  • → Yes, I've tried it once or twice
  • → No, but I'd be open to it
  • → No, not something I'd seek out
Q6 — Spend & Dwell Time
When you visit one of these places to read, study, work, or meet up, what do you typically spend per visit and how long do you usually stay?
Broadened from Katy's coffee-shop-only version. Adds a $0 option for free places like libraries and makes it relevant to the full audience.
  • → Spend: $0 / Under $5 / $5–$10 / $10–$15 / $15+
  • → Time: Under 30 min / 30–60 min / 1–2 hours / 2+ hours
Section 3 — Reading & Book Communities
Q7 — Reading Communities & Platforms
Are you part of any reading communities or platforms, online or in person? Select all that apply.
Katy's original combined two separate questions. This version separates community membership from event attendance and expands to include Reddit and Substack.
  • → BookTok / TikTok reading community
  • → Bookstagram / Goodreads / StoryGraph
  • → Audible / Libby
  • → Reddit reading communities / Substack newsletters
  • → Local or private book club / Library reading group
  • → None of the above
Section 4 — Experience & Atmosphere
Q9 — Vibe Meter
On a scale of 1–10, how much does the atmosphere of a place influence whether you go back?
Tightened from Katy's original. Validates that atmosphere is a real differentiator. If scores skew high, that's investor-ready proof.
  • → 1 = I barely notice it  /  10 = It's the whole reason I go
Q10 — Madison Gap
Is there a type of place or experience you wish Madison had for reading, studying, or spending time with friends?
A sleeper question. Unprompted signal about a reading spot or plant-based drinks is gold for investor conversations. Open field to avoid leading.
  • → Open field
Section 5 — About You
Q11 — Neighborhood
Which area do you live in or spend the most time in?
Geographic clustering helps map demand by neighborhood and informs location decisions.

Q12 — Age Range
Which of the following best describes you?
Validates target personas (Gen Z, Millennials, Gen X) and helps cluster responses by generation.
  • → Under 18 / 18–24 / 25–34 / 35–44 / 45–54 / 55+
Q13 — Student Status
Are you currently a student?
Students are a core segment. Quick yes/no lets Katy segment responses without adding friction.
  • → Yes / No
Q14 — Email Capture & Incentive
As a thank you for taking the survey, we'd love to give you half off your first drink at Lucky Crow when it opens, plus a free premium sticker.
Converts survey respondents into an early-access list. The incentive rewards participation and creates a reason to engage at opening.
  • → Yes, sign me up (email field)
  • → No thanks
  • → Optional: Add me to the Lucky Crow newsletter and event invites

PART 4 — DISTRIBUTION

Getting it in front of people

Five channels identified. We recommend keeping it to three to stay focused and manageable.

01
Guerrilla Flyers

Printed flyers with QR code. One hook, one image or logo, one big QR code. Nobody stops to read a paragraph on a telephone pole.

Recommended locations: State Street, University Ave, Willy Street, Atwood, Monroe Street, Williamson/Jenifer corridor, independent coffee shops, yoga studios, bookstores, Willy Street Co-op, wellness boutiques.

Recommend 2–3 versions with different hooks: Lotus hook (wellness crowd) and book/reading hook (BookTok crowd).

⚠ Flyer copy and design to be developed by Big Sis. Some locations (especially UW campus) have posting rules — verify before distributing.
02
Facebook Book Community Groups

Katy is already a member of several large book communities, some with 50,000+ members.

Post as a community member, not as a brand. Check group rules before posting. Some groups allow native polls — use as a softer entry point before dropping the survey link.

03
TikTok / BookTok

BookTok is massive, highly engaged, and perfectly aligned with Lucky Crow's book community audience.

Short authentic video from Katy — 30 seconds, genuine and personal. Survey link in bio. Authentic works better than polished on TikTok.

Note: TikTok's future in the US has been uncertain. Worth noting as a platform risk.
04
Meta Ads (optional)

Pending audience research. Instagram likely outperforms Facebook for the 25–44 demographic.

Start with a small test budget of $25–$50 before scaling. Target by interest: books, reading, plant-based lifestyle, wellness, Madison WI.

⚠ Meta ad audience research to be completed by Big Sis before recommending a budget to Katy.
05
Reddit (research only)

The Madison subreddit is active but rejects anything that feels promotional. Don't post the survey link directly. Use Reddit for research and sentiment — read existing threads about Madison coffee shops and third spaces.

Recommended distribution stack

  • → Channel 1: Guerrilla flyers with QR codes in target neighborhoods (2–3 versions)
  • → Channel 2: Facebook book community groups — organic posts by Katy as community member
  • → Channel 3: TikTok/BookTok — short authentic video from Katy
  • → Channel 4 (optional): One paid Meta ad, small test budget, Instagram-first, pending audience research

Topics

Let's talk

01
Approach

The Mom Test is a question framework that avoids pitching your idea while gathering feedback — instead of asking "would you use this?" (which invites polite lies), it asks about real past behavior and actual problems people have already experienced. The goal is to surface honest signal, not validation.

We will also be using format, set-up language and survey-length best practices to ensure people see the survey through.

02
What are we trying to learn?
  1. If this survey came back tomorrow with 100 responses, what are the three things you'd most want to know?
  2. Is there anything about your customers or concept that you're not sure about yet? Is there anything that would change if some of your assumptions don't align?
  3. With such a unique concept, are you okay learning separately about needs for a local plant-based apothecary and for a special place to get lost in for events, reading or work?
  4. For the feedback you received about "Traction," what type of information do you think the investors or lenders want to see?
  5. Beyond email addresses, what data points would actually help you: demographics, location, beverage habits, event interest, willingness to pay?
  6. Are there things you feel confident enough about that you don't need to survey for them — so we don't waste questions?
03
Who are we asking?
  1. Do you have an existing audience anywhere or are you part of any groups you'd like to tap into? IRL or digital.
  2. Who would you like to hear from? Readers, students, people who live in certain neighborhoods, etc?
  3. Are there any groups you specifically don't want to skew toward?
04
What's the trade for filling out the survey?
  1. What can we offer someone for a few minutes of their time? Win a prize, a free drink, invite to grand opening, branded cup, etc?
  2. In addition to capturing email addresses for a newsletter, how else can we take advantage of engaged potential customers?
05
How does the survey get in front of people?
  1. We've discussed flyers, online groups, email lists and more. Let's identify 2–3 approaches that will yield the best results.
  2. Are there any communities, organizations, or venues in Madison you already have a relationship with that might help share it?
  3. Would you be comfortable doing any cold or blind outreach, including posting in Madison Reddit, Facebook groups, or UW forums?
