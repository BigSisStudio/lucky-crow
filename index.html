Survey Review & Recommendations

Lucky Crow Apothecary & Readeasy™ · Presentation 02 · 06.09.2026

QUESTIONS

Gathering the good stuff

Remember: All questions are written using The Mom Test, a framework that asks about real past behavior instead of hypotheticals, so the responses reflect what people actually do, not what they aspire to do or what they think you want to hear.
Section 1 — The Third Space
Q1a, Where Do You Go?
When you need to get out of the house to read, study, work, or meet up with someone, where do you go in Madison? Select all that apply.
Opens with a behavior-first anchor. Captures where people actually go today rather than hypothetical preferences. Sets up context for Q2 and Q3.
  • → Coffee shop or café
  • → Library
  • → Bookstore
  • → Restaurant
  • → Juice bar
  • → Other (please specify)
Q1b, Frequency
How often do you visit these types of places?
Frequency data reveals how habitual these visits are. A weekly visitor is a very different customer than a monthly one.
  • → More than once a week
  • → Once a week
  • → Every other week
  • → Once a month
  • → Less than once a month
Q2, What Brings You Back?
What brings you back to a place? Select up to three.
Inspiration: "When you want to read, study, or work outside of your home/office in Madison, where do you usually go, and what is the #1 thing that ruins that experience for you or makes you leave?"
Flipping to what brings you back captures a fuller picture of what people value most. Asking about the negative primes frustration; this version surfaces real loyalty drivers. Capping at three forces prioritization and makes data more actionable.
  • → Food or beverage quality
  • → Food or beverage selection
  • → Noise level
  • → Vibe and atmosphere
  • → Decor & design
  • → Staff knowledge
  • → Staff friendliness
  • → Price
  • → Cleanliness
  • → Kid-friendly
  • → Outdoor seating
  • → Comfortable seating
  • → Privacy
  • → Other (please specify)
Q3, Travel Distance
How far are you willing to travel regularly for a spot you love?
Helps understand realistic catchment area and informs location decisions. Madison residents can be very neighborhood-loyal.
  • → Within walking distance
  • → Under 10 minutes
  • → 10–20 minutes
  • → I'd go across town
Section 2 — Drinks & Caffeine Habits
Q4, Caffeine Routine
During a typical week, what does your caffeine routine look like?
Inspiration: "How often do you consume energy drinks or non-coffee caffeine compared to traditional coffee? (Daily, 2-3 times a week, Rarely, Nevarrr! Coffee for life!)"
Separates frequency and preference, and distinguishes traditional energy drinks from plant-based, critical for understanding where the audience is today vs. where Lucky Crow takes them. Removes the humorous answer options that could skew tone and data quality.
  • → Mostly coffee
  • → Mostly traditional energy drinks (Red Bull, Monster, etc.)
  • → Mostly plant-based energy drinks (Lotus, Guayakí, etc.)
  • → Mostly tea
  • → Mix of different types
  • → I avoid caffeine entirely
  • → Other
Q5a — Plant-Based Familiarity
Are you familiar with plant-based or botanical energy drinks, like Lotus, Guayakí, or others?
Establishes baseline awareness before asking about experience. Prevents people who have never heard of these products from feeling lost on Q5b, and helps segment responses by familiarity level.
  • → Yes, I know them well
  • → I've heard of them but don't know much
  • → No, this is new to me
Q5b — Plant-Based Experience
Have you ever tried a plant-based or botanical energy drink, like Lotus, Guayakí, or others?
Inspiration: "If you were offered a premium, plant-based energy drink (clean caffeine and added benefits), would you choose it over your standard coffee or soda/energy drink? (Definitely/No/Maybe)"
The original asks a hypothetical that primes a yes. This version asks about past behavior first, which surfaces more honest signal and less social desirability bias.
  • → Yes, it's a regular thing for me
  • → Yes, I've tried it once or twice
  • → No, but I'd be open to it
  • → No, not something I'd seek out
Q6, Spend & Dwell Time
When you go somewhere to read, study, work, or meet up, what do you typically spend and how long do you usually stay?
Inspiration: "What do you typically spend each time you visit a local café/coffee shop, and how long do you usually stay?"
Broadened beyond coffee shops to match the full range of places captured in Q1. Adds a $0 option for free spaces like libraries, making it relevant to the whole audience.
  • → Spend: $0 / Under $5 / $5–$10 / $10–$15 / $15+
  • → Time: Under 30 min / 30–60 min / 1–2 hours / 2+ hours
Section 3 — Reading & Book Communities
Q7, Reading Communities & Platforms
Are you part of any reading communities or platforms, online or in person? Select all that apply.
Inspiration: "Are you a part of an online reading community/group (BookTok, Bookstagram, Goodreads, StoryGraph, Audible, Libby, etc.)? If so, how often do you attend local book-related events or meetups? If you don't attend any because there are none in your area that you're aware of, would you attend these kinds of events if they happened more regularly in your area?"
The original combines three separate questions into one, which makes it hard to analyze. This version focuses on community membership and expands to include Reddit and Substack. Event attendance is captured separately where relevant.
  • → BookTok / TikTok reading community
  • → Bookstagram / Goodreads / StoryGraph
  • → Audible / Libby
  • → Reddit reading communities / Substack newsletters
  • → Local or private book club / Library reading group
  • → None
Section 4 — Experience & Atmosphere
Q8, Vibe Meter
On a scale of 1–10, how much does the atmosphere of a place influence whether you go back?
Inspiration: "On a scale of 1-10, how much does the experience or atmosphere of a space influence your choice of where to spend your time? (1 being 'I don't even think about it' and 10 being 'I won't waste my time unless there's a vibe!')"
Tightened to remove the humorous scale descriptors, which can skew responses. Focuses the question on return behavior rather than initial choice, which is more predictive. If scores skew high, that's investor-ready proof.
  • → 1 = I barely notice it  /  10 = It's the whole reason I go
Q9, Madison Gap
Is there a type of place or experience you wish Madison had for reading, studying, or spending time with friends?
A sleeper question. Unprompted signal about a reading spot or plant-based drinks is gold for investor conversations. Open field to avoid leading.
  • → Open field
Section 5 — About You
Q10, Neighborhood
Which area do you live in or spend the most time in?
Geographic clustering helps map demand by neighborhood and informs location decisions.
  • → Downtown/IsthmusCampus, Capitol Square/Downtown, Tenney-Lapham
  • → Campus Edge & Near WestRegent/Greenbush/Vilas, Dudgeon-Monroe, Crawford/Marlborough/Nakoma, Westmorland, Shorewood Hills, Midvale Heights/Hilldale
  • → Near East SideMarquette (Willy Street), Schenk-Atwood, Eken Park/Hawthorne, Grandview Commons/Far East Side, Glendale/Elvehjem
  • → NorthsideSherman, Cherokee Park, Brentwood Village/Berkley Oaks, Maple Bluff
  • → South & Far WestBay Creek, South Madison, Wexford Ridge/High Point, Arbor Hills, Allied Drive, Leopold
  • → Nearby SuburbsVerona, Middleton, Fitchburg, Monona, Cottage Grove, Sun Prairie
  • → Other (please specify)
Q11, Age Range
Which of the following best describes you?
Validates target personas (Gen Z, Millennials, Gen X) and helps cluster responses by generation.
  • → Under 18 / 18–24 / 25–34 / 35–44 / 45–54 / 55+
Q12, Student Status
Are you currently a student?
Students are a core segment. Quick yes/no lets Katy segment responses without adding friction.
  • → Yes / No
Q13, Email Capture & Incentive
As a thank you for completing the survey, we'd love to give you half off your first drink and a free premium sticker at our new space when it opens.
Converts survey respondents into an early-access list. The incentive rewards participation and creates a reason to engage at opening.
  • → Yes, sign me up (email field)
  • → No thanks
  • → Optional: Add me to our newsletter and event invites
Note: By providing your email address, you may also receive news updates and invitations from us.
⚠ Confirm compliance with Wisconsin sweepstakes and incentive laws before launching.
PLATFORM

Survey selection

We explored multiple platforms based on the criteria we discussed.

Tally
  • → Free, unlimited forms and submissions
  • → Clean, mobile-first interface with no branding on free plan
  • → Native email capture, QR code generation, and conditional logic
Typeform
  • → Conversational one-question-at-a-time format
  • → ~47% avg completion rate vs 21.5% industry average
  • → Free tier caps at 10 responses/month; $50/mo to remove branding
SurveyMonkey
  • → Widely recognized and easy to use
  • → Strong analytics and reporting tools
  • → Free plan caps at 25 responses per survey
Google Forms
  • → Free and unlimited responses
  • → Integrates directly with Google Sheets
  • → No branding options, generic appearance
Our recommendation:
Tally

Here's why it meets our requirements:

  • → Unlimited responses on the free plan, no caps to worry about as distribution scales
  • → Automatically switch the reward offer off after a set number of redemptions, so the incentive cap manages itself
  • → Generates a shareable link natively
  • → Supports all question types needed: multiple choice, scale, open field, two-part, and email capture
  • → All response data exports cleanly to a spreadsheet for easy management and segmentation
  • → Easy template sharing and handoff

Additional features to consider with Pro ($29/month)

  • → Creative flexibility to match the visual tone of the brand
  • → Capturing partial submissions
  • → Automated "Thank You" email

Setting the Stage

Just getting started

This session is an opportunity to discuss ways to make the survey as impactful as possible. No questions, design or formats will be reviewed today, but that'll be coming soon.

  • → Working session to define what we're trying to learn
  • → Chance to identify the right audience and where/how to reach them
  • → Conversation about what makes someone want to respond
  • → Alignment on what we need from our survey platform
  • → Identify what info we still need before launching the survey
Topics

Let's talk

01
Approach

The Mom Test is a question framework that avoids pitching your idea while gathering feedback, instead of asking "would you use this?" (which invites polite lies), it asks about real past behavior and actual problems people have already experienced. The goal is to surface honest signal, not validation.

We will also be using format, set-up language and survey-length best practices to ensure people see the survey through.

02
What are we trying to learn?
  1. → If this survey came back tomorrow with 100 responses, what are the three things you'd most want to know?
  2. → Is there anything about your customers or concept that you're not sure about yet? Is there anything that would change if some of your assumptions don't align?
  3. → With such a unique concept, are you okay learning separately about needs for a local plant-based apothecary and for a special place to get lost in for events, reading or work?
  4. → For the feedback you received about "Traction," what type of information do you think the investors or lenders want to see?
  5. → Beyond email addresses, what data points would actually help you: demographics, location, beverage habits, event interest, willingness to pay?
  6. → Are there things you feel confident enough about that you don't need to survey for them, so we don't waste questions?
03
Who are we asking?
  1. → Do you have an existing audience anywhere or are you part of any groups you'd like to tap into? IRL or digital.
  2. → Who would you like to hear from? Readers, students, people who live in certain neighborhoods, etc?
  3. → Are there any groups you specifically don't want to skew toward?
04
What's the trade for filling out the survey?
  1. → What can we offer someone for a few minutes of their time? Win a prize, a free drink, invite to grand opening, branded cup, etc?
  2. → In addition to capturing email addresses for a newsletter, how else can we take advantage of engaged potential customers?
05
How does the survey get in front of people?
  1. → We've discussed flyers, online groups, email lists and more. Let's identify 2–3 approaches that will yield the best results.
  2. → Are there any communities, organizations, or venues in Madison you already have a relationship with that might help share it?
  3. → Would you be comfortable doing any cold or blind outreach, including posting in Madison Reddit, Facebook groups, or UW forums?
On the Horizon

What's next
