How we looked at it

Logo Evaluation Framework

Decades of brand work has taught us what a logo needs to do, beyond look good. We built this framework to check those boxes as objectively as possible, so your full logo system accurately represents the Lucky Crow brand, no matter the context.

  • Memory, Could someone reproduce the essence of this after seeing it once?
  • Belonging, Does it feel true to this specific brand, and only this brand?
  • Legibility, Can you read it everywhere it needs to be read?
  • Simplification, What survives when you strip it down, and does that core travel?
  • Art or Error, Does it hold together as a designed thing, or does it read as assembled?

What's working

Strong Base

Good news! The two logo attributes that performed best are the two that are the hardest to infuse into a logo. That means you have a memorable concept as the basis of your system and a strong foundation to build upon. Let's look at the results.

Pass
Memory
Could someone reproduce the essence of this after seeing it once?
  • Immediately describable: "Black crow in front of a crescent moon" is a clear signature.
  • Not trend-dependent: This draws from a visual tradition with real longevity.
  • The version that includes Apothecary & Readeasy will also draw curiosity.
Pass
Belonging
Does it feel true to this specific brand, and only this brand?
  • It's specific: This mark cannot be swapped onto a competitor without immediate confusion.
  • Communicates something real: Mystical, nature-rooted, slightly literary, independent.
  • The crow: Intelligence, luck, mystery. It's the right symbol for what Lucky Crow is.
What needs work

Fixable Fails

The idea and design approach are there, but we need to address the functionality of the logo. The goal is to ensure your brand is identifiable in every single use case. There's room for improvement through slight modifications and an expanded family of marks and variations.

Fail
Legibility
Can you read it everywhere it needs to be read?
Where it breaks down
  • Name legibility: Doesn't read at 1" wide in print or 80px on screen. At business card scale, the logo would likely need to fill the entire card to be legible.
  • Apothecary & Readeasy: These are your differentiators, but they disappear below 2" wide and are difficult to read on dark backgrounds.
  • Fine detail strokes: At standard sizes, fine details become hard to read and even harder to reproduce. Use cases like embroidery may not be viable without simplification.
  • Accessibility: The gold doesn't pass ADA contrast compliance against the dark background, making it difficult to read for some users.
What's working
  • Fully vector: No raster elements, clean foundation to build from.
  • Color count: A small, fixed number that serves your logo well.
Fail
Simplification
What survives when you strip it down, and does that core travel?
Where it breaks down
  • Color dependency: The crow relies on black/white stroke contrast to read as a bird. In flat black, it reads as a solid shape, not a logo.
  • Lack of simple option: Key elements of the logo don't have reduced versions for specific uses like stamps, one-color production, sponsorship logos, and more.
  • Creative use of elements: Between the crow, moon, lotus, and wordmark, building icon and logo options that reimagine how those elements work together would expand what the system can do.
What's working
  • Strong anchor: The crow, moon, lotus, and wordmark composition is ownable. The simplification work isn't about removing anything. It's about rethinking how to use all the components.
Fail
Art or Error
Does it hold together as a designed thing?
Where it breaks down
  • Hierarchy is inverted: The brand name is an important element of any logo, and right now it's the hardest thing to make out.
  • Inconsistent registers: The crow and lotus are drawn with different line weights and stylization, making it hard for the eye to settle.
What's working
  • Every element is valuable: The crow, the moon, the lotus. This isn't random. It's a coherent visual world that needs its elements to align in execution, not a different concept.
  • On theme: Mystical, nature-rooted, slightly literary. The palette supports this energy.

Where we go from here

Building Out the System

01
Commit to a visual language

The crow, the lotus, the moon need to speak the same language — same line weight, same stylization, same attitude. Right now they're in different registers. Unify them and the mark reads as designed rather than assembled.

02
Balanced hierarchy

"Lucky Crow" is the most important piece of information in this mark. It needs to hold at every size, not disappear behind the illustration. Name and imagery need equal weight in the composition.

03
A simplified digital mark

A version stripped down to the essential visual signature — something that lives at small sizes, on social profiles, as a favicon, on merch. Not a replacement for the full mark. A complement to it.

04
A true one-color version

Solid fills, no stroke dependency. Opens up embroidery, screen print, stamps, and reversed-out applications. A gap the current system doesn't address.

Goals for the digital mark

Digital Checklist

01
Pass at 110×110px

Social profile images display at approximately 110px on most platforms. That's the primary target. The mark needs to be immediately recognizable: brand name legible, key visual elements reading clearly, at that size without zooming in.

02
Visual balance

Right now the crow and moon carry the top half; the lotus and name band carry the bottom, but they're not in visual conversation. Bringing them into balance so the mark reads as a unified composition.

03
Cohesive illustration style

The crow and the lotus are currently drawn in different registers. The digital version works toward a consistent visual language across both, same weight, same stylization level, same attitude.

04
Improved contrast

This isn't about ADA compliance for logos — it's about legibility. The mark needs to read. As elements shrink and detail gets stripped, contrast becomes the thing holding recognition together. Higher contrast = the mark registers at 110px without confusion.

05
Reduce to essential elements

At small scale, every element that isn't doing critical work becomes noise. The digital version strips back to what needs to be there and nothing more, so what remains can be read clearly.
